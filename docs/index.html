It knows the time

A viewpoint scores highest at sunset, a shaded restaurant at the hottest part of the day, a market in the evening. Anything that will not fit before you leave is hidden rather than listed.

📅

It builds the day

Pick places and they become an ordered plan with times on it. It waits rather than sending you somewhere four hours early, and it says when something collides with opening hours instead of quietly moving it.

🗺

Written by people

Places and opening hours from OpenStreetMap. Descriptions, prices and safety notes from Wikivoyage. Sunrise worked out on your own device. Not a sentence of it was generated.

📴

Your saved trips work with no signal

Add it to your home screen and it behaves like an app. A town you have opened stays on your phone, not on a server, because there is no server. Searching a new town, and the map's background tiles, still need a connection.

Where it is good, and where it is thin

The guide is only as good as what has been mapped, and that varies a lot. In central Lisbon a third of places carry opening hours. In a small town in Rajasthan it is closer to one in twenty.

So it tells you which one you are in, shows hours unknown rather than guessing, and points you at OpenStreetMap if you want to fix your own town, which helps everyone rather than one app.

What this knows about you

Nothing that identifies you, and nothing about where you searched.

Your trip is saved in this browser and nowhere else. It is not sent to us, because there is no us: this is a static page with no server behind it. Clearing your browser data deletes it and we could not recover it if you asked.

The town you type is sent, as a search, to the public services that answer the question: OpenStreetMap (Nominatim and Overpass) for places and their opening hours, Wikivoyage for descriptions, Wikidata and Wikimedia Commons for photographs, Open-Meteo and MET Norway for the forecast, and Photon if Nominatim is down. Each of those sees a request from your browser, as it would from any site that asked them. Nothing is sent to us, because there is no us, and none of it is stored outside your device.

This paragraph used to name only OpenStreetMap and Wikivoyage, and claimed the town went to nobody else. That was wrong, and on this page of all pages. The list above is the whole of it, and you can check it by opening your browser's network tab.

Counting is currently off entirely. If it is ever switched on it will be a cookieless counter recording which screens get opened, never what you typed or where you are. You can check that claim in the source, which is the only kind of privacy promise worth anything.

03 Local time

Guessed UTC from longitude. The guide runs on the destination's clock, not your phone's, which matters if you've just landed and your phone hasn't caught up.

It's an estimate from where the place sits east or west, not a lookup. Half-hour zones like India are handled; oddities may not be.
