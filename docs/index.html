01 How this works

Everything happens in this tab. Your browser asks OpenStreetMap what's nearby and when it opens, asks Wikivoyage what travellers have written about those places, and works out sunrise and sunset from orbital maths on your own device.

There is no account, no server of ours, no API key, and no AI. Every sentence you'll read about a place was written by a person, not generated.

Once it's loaded it works offline, and you can add it to your home screen like an app. Nothing is sent anywhere; the trip is stored only on this device.

02 What it's for

You land somewhere with a few hours. A normal guide lists two hundred things with no sense of time. Half are shut, some are an hour away, a few take longer than you've got. This one answers a narrower question: it's 3pm and I leave at 7, so what now?

02 Local time

Guessed UTC from longitude. The guide runs on the destination's clock, not your phone's, which matters if you've just landed and your phone hasn't caught up.

It's an estimate from where the place sits east or west, not a lookup. Half-hour zones like India are handled; oddities may not be.
